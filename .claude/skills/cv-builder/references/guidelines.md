# CV Writing Guidelines

Synthesised from:
- National Careers Service (UK) — https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections
- Modèles de CV — https://www.modeles-de-cv.com/faire-un-cv/
- Andrew Lambert, "Guide to writing a CV" — https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/guide-writing-cv-andrew-lambert-xkgbe/

## Purpose
A CV is a summary of your skills, achievements and experience — the first chance to impress. The goal is not to recount your whole life but to let a recruiter understand **quickly what you can bring to this role**.

## Tailor every CV (the single most important rule — all three sources)
- Read the job description; mirror its title and keywords where they genuinely apply.
- Put the most relevant skills/experience first; trim what doesn't serve the role.
- Keep one CV version per application (folder per company).

## Length
- Student / recent graduate / little experience: **1 page**.
- Long career or academic/specialised background: 2 pages (Lambert accepts up to 3 for senior profiles in the UK).
- Only pertinent information.

## Layout & formatting
- Clear font (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman or a clean serif/sans), **10–12pt** body, black text; consistent styling throughout.
- Headings, bold, italics and bullet points for structure. Use real lists/formatting — never spaces for alignment.
- Professional, restrained colours. No overlapping design elements, no complex tables.
- PDF with **selectable text**.

## Sections (typical order)
1. **Contact details** — name, phone, professional email, city, LinkedIn/GitHub/portfolio. Omit age, date of birth, marital status, nationality.
2. **Title** — the role you target (matching the posting).
3. **Introduction / professional summary** — a few sentences under the name: who you are, key strengths matching the posting, career aim. Mention professional qualifications here.
4. **Work experience** — reverse chronological. Employer, job title, dates (month + year), location, then 2–6 bullets of responsibilities **and achievements**. Placements, internships and volunteering count.
5. **Education** — reverse chronological. Qualification, institution, dates, grades/honours, relevant projects. Put it **before** experience if early in career and experience is thin; after it otherwise.
6. **Skills** — split into technical skills, methods, tools, languages, soft skills. Avoid generic trait lists ("motivated, dynamic"); prove soft skills through experience bullets.
7. **Languages** — honest levels (don't overstate; CEFR levels A1–C2 are fine).
8. **Projects / certifications / achievements** not covered elsewhere.
9. **Interests** — optional, short; useful as an interview conversation starter.
10. **References** — never list contacts; "available on request" (optional).

## Writing bullets
- Formula: **action verb + task + context + measurable result**.
  - Weak: "Managed social media."
  - Strong: "Planned and published 20 LinkedIn posts per month, growing engagement 35% over six months."
- Use the **STAR** method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) to find the content, then compress to one line.
- Start with strong past-tense verbs (FR: Conçu, Développé, Livré, Automatisé, Déployé, Optimisé, Sécurisé, Mis en place, Réduit, Migré; EN: Designed, Built, Delivered, Automated, Deployed, Optimised, Secured, Implemented, Reduced, Migrated).
- Quantify wherever true: counts (endpoints, users, entities), % improvements, time saved, team size, frequency.

## Adapting by profile
| Profile | Emphasise |
|---|---|
| Student / recent graduate | Education, internships, projects, transferable skills |
| Career changer | New training, transferable skills, clear transition logic |
| Experienced | Recent achievements; summarise older roles |

First job: highlight transferable skills from projects, volunteering, hobbies (leadership, organisation). Employment gaps are normal — explain briefly if asked, don't hide with fake dates.

## ATS (applicant tracking systems)
- Standard section titles ("Expériences professionnelles", "Formation", "Compétences" / "Experience", "Education", "Skills").
- Logical single-column reading order, clear chronology, consistent date format.
- No information carried only by icons or images.
- Include the posting's keywords naturally — only where they match real experience.

## Photo
Optional in France (recent, sharp, professional; no selfies/filters/cluttered background). Avoid in UK, US, Canada.

## Using AI
AI may help rephrase, but every claim must be verified and generic phrasing replaced with specific, real examples. **Never fabricate skills or results.**

## Common mistakes
- Unrelated information; vague bullets with no outcome.
- Fabricated skills, results or language levels.
- Missing/incorrect phone or email.
- Spaces used for alignment; broken layout in PDF.
- Generic file name ("CV.pdf") or generic title ("Curriculum Vitae").
- Spelling and grammar errors — proofread (and ideally have someone else read it).

## Pre-submission checklist
- [ ] Title matches the posting.
- [ ] Contact details correct (email, phone, city, links work).
- [ ] Summary tailored to this role.
- [ ] Experience and education in reverse chronological order with consistent date format.
- [ ] Every bullet starts with an action verb; results quantified where true.
- [ ] Posting keywords present where genuinely applicable; gaps reported, not invented.
- [ ] No personal data (age, DOB, marital status, nationality) unless requested.
- [ ] Length respected (1 page for this profile); nothing cut off.
- [ ] Spelling/grammar/accents checked; no glued words (e.g. "unstage" → "un stage").
- [ ] PDF text selectable; professional filename `FIRSTNAME_LASTNAME_CV_Company.pdf`.
- [ ] Opened and reviewed the final PDF.
